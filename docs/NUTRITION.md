# Nutrition and the body profile

A food diary beside the training log: what you ate, how much energy and protein / fat /
carbohydrate it carried, and how the day stands against a target. It is part of the same
profile as everything else — it works offline, in guest mode and in the phone app, syncs like
a workout, and is in the JSON export.

It is on by default and can be switched off per profile (Settings → **Nutrition**), which hides
the Home card and the screen and keeps the data.

Three parts of it are **optional and off until an admin turns them on**, because each moves
data across a boundary: looking products up online, asking an AI about a photo, and receiving
body data from Apple Health. The diary, the targets and the built-in food list need none of
them.

## Logging food

- **The built-in list** — 438 everyday foods with Russian and English names, searchable by
  either ("гречку", "chicken breast"). Values are per 100 g.
- **My products** — anything you add yourself, from a label. Saved once, found by name after.
- **Recent** — the last things you logged, with the weight you used last time.
- **Quick add** — bare numbers, for a restaurant meal you only know the calories of.
- **Copy the day before** — on an empty day.

Every logged row keeps its own numbers. Correcting a product later does not rewrite what last
month added up to.

### Where the built-in list comes from

`frontend/src/lib/foods-data.js` is generated by `scripts/build-foods.mjs` from two inputs in
`scripts/food-sources/`:

| File | What | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| `selection.json` | 367 rows of **USDA FoodData Central, SR Legacy (April 2018)**, each with the Russian name, short English name and search aliases written for this project | values: public domain (CC0); names: this project's |
| `supplement.json` | 71 foods that table lacks — tvorog, kefir, pelmeni, borscht … — typical values, hand-authored | this project's; **approximate** |

The archive is pinned by SHA-256; the script fails if the source changes or if a row's USDA
description no longer matches its id. Regenerate with `node scripts/build-foods.mjs`, verify
with `--check`. Ids (`u<fdc id>`, `s-<slug>`) are stable — never renumber one that shipped.

A home-made borscht is whatever went into the pot. The supplement rows are a starting point,
not a measurement.

### Keeping the profile small

The diary is the busiest list in a profile, and the whole profile is sent on every change. So
days older than **90 days are folded into one daily total each** (energy, macros, number of
entries) and their individual rows are dropped. Totals, charts and averages are unaffected;
what you can no longer do is open a four-month-old day and see what the entries were.

## Targets

Settings are under the target button on the Nutrition screen.

**My own numbers** — you type kcal and grams; nothing is computed.

**Calculated** — from the body profile (Body screen):

| Step | Formula |
|---|---|
| Resting expenditure | Mifflin–St Jeor (`10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + 5` / `− 161`); Katch–McArdle (`370 + 21.6·lean kg`) when body fat is known |
| Daily expenditure | × activity factor 1.2 / 1.375 / 1.55 / 1.725 / 1.9 |
| Goal | lose 0.25–1 % or gain 0.1–0.5 % of body weight a week, at 7700 kcal per kg |
| Protein | 1.2–2.4 g per kg (default 1.8) |
| Fat | at least 0.7 g per kg, about a quarter of energy |
| Carbohydrate | what is left |

The target **never goes below 1200 kcal (women) / 1500 kcal (men or unspecified)**; when the
arithmetic would, the app says the floor was applied.

**Measured instead of estimated.** Once there are at least 10 fully-logged days and 8 weigh-ins
spread over the last four weeks, expenditure is computed from the diary itself:

```
expenditure = mean daily intake − (weight change per day × 7700)
```

and blended with the formula (half and half; four-fifths measured with 20 logged days and 12
weigh-ins). No watch is involved — wrist devices miss energy expenditure by 30 % and more. With
too little data the app keeps the formula and says so; it does not guess.

**Following your training.** With the switch on, a day you train gets the energy of that
session minus the daily average of your sessions, and a rest day gives it back — at most ±15 %,
moved into carbohydrate. Over a normal week it adds up to the same total. A session is priced
from its logged duration (3 kcal per kg per hour above resting), not from a wearable.

These are estimates for healthy adults. The app says so on the target sheet, and it is not a
substitute for a dietitian.

## Online lookup (Open Food Facts)

Off by default. Admin dashboard → **Nutrition & Health** → *Online food lookup*.
`FOOD_LOOKUP_DISABLED=1` in the environment keeps it off whatever the dashboard says.

When on, a member can type the digits under a barcode, or a product name, and press a button
to look it up. The request goes **from the server**, not from the phone:

- what leaves is the barcode or the search phrase — never who asked;
- the server identifies itself to Open Food Facts with a `User-Agent` naming this instance
  (set a contact address in the dashboard; their terms ask for one);
- answers are cached in `data/off-cache.json` (products 90 days, "not found" 7 days, searches
  24 hours), so most lookups after the first never leave the server;
- the database's own limits (15 product reads, 10 searches a minute per address) are kept
  under for the whole instance, with a smaller limit per member.

There is no camera barcode scanner on iPhone: Safari has no barcode API, and adding a decoder
library was decided against. Type the digits, or photograph the label (below).

Coverage of products sold in Russia is thin — about 17,000 with complete nutrition. A miss is
normal; the app then offers the product form.

Data from Open Food Facts is under the **Open Database License**; the app credits it wherever
it is shown, and the cache is kept in its own file for that reason. A product you save from a
lookup becomes one of your own products, with its source recorded.

## The AI: a photo, a label, a description, "what should I eat?"

Needs the AI Coach to be set up on the instance (see [AI_COACH.md](AI_COACH.md)); nothing here
appears otherwise.

| Entry point | What is sent | What comes back |
|---|---|---|
| **Photo of the meal** | the photo (scaled to 1024 px JPEG) and your caption | a list of foods with weights — a draft |
| **Describe in words** | what you typed | the same draft |
| **Photo of the label** | the photo | per-100 g values, opened in the product form |
| **What should I eat?** → *Ask the AI* | what is left of today's target, the names and per-100 g values of up to 40 foods you eat often, and your wish | a few meals with weights |

Nothing else from the diary, and nothing from the training log. The photo is held in the
server's memory for the length of the call and is not written to disk, to the profile, or to
the log (`api/test/food-ai.test.js` asserts that).

It has **its own consent** — agreeing to the Coach does not cover it — and its own daily cap
(30 requests per profile by default; dashboard → AI Coach → Advanced).

**Everything it returns is a draft.** A published comparison on standardised photos found
the best models off by about 36 % on energy and 40–70 % on macros, worse for large portions; a
local model will do worse. So the draft sheet says so, every weight is editable, and nothing
is logged until you press Add. Two things improve it a lot:

- a caption ("buckwheat with chicken, plate about 300 g");
- the built-in table: when the model names a food the table has, the table's per-100 g values
  are used instead of the model's, unless the two disagree wildly.

"What should I eat?" always works without any AI: it sizes portions of foods you already eat
to what is left of the target (`frontend/src/lib/food-suggest.js`).

### A local model

Photos need a provider that can be sent pictures: the API providers and any
OpenAI-compatible server (Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM). The subscription CLIs cannot; with them
only *Describe in words* is offered.

With Ollama or LM Studio the vision model is usually a different one from the text model, so
it has its own field: dashboard → AI Coach → Model → **Photos (food diary)**. Press **Test
photo reading** after choosing it — the server shows the model a red square and checks that
it says "red". A model that answers without having seen the picture is the failure this
catches; nothing else can.

Requests are queued and polled rather than awaited, so a slow model does not run into a
proxy's 60-second timeout. `COACH_JOB_TIMEOUT_MS` bounds one request.

## The body profile

Body screen (Home → Nutrition → *Open body profile*, or Settings → **Body profile**):

- sex, year of birth, height, everyday activity — each optional, each used only for the
  energy estimate. "Not set" for sex puts the estimate between the two formulas.
- measurements by date (waist, chest, hips, arms, thighs, calves, body fat), in centimetres
  or inches after the profile's unit, charted over time.
- the Apple Health connection — see [HEALTH_SHORTCUT.md](HEALTH_SHORTCUT.md).
- **Share with the Coach** — two switches, off by default. With the Coach's ordinary consent
  it sees neither. Switched on, it receives a *summary*: age (not birth year), sex, height,
  activity, body fat and how each measurement moved; and for nutrition the target and
  two-week averages — never a food name, never a photo. The Coach is told to use this to judge
  recovery and progress, and not to prescribe a diet.

The figure the muscle map is drawn on (Settings → Appearance) is a drawing preference and is
never read as anyone's sex.

## What is stored where

| Data | Where | In a backup of `./data` |
|---|---|---|
| diary, my products, targets, body profile, measurements | the profile (`state-<uid>.json`) | yes |
| Open Food Facts cache | `off-cache.json` | yes — safe to delete |
| the two admin switches | `settings.json` | yes |
| Apple Health deliveries and token hashes | `health/<uid>.json`, `health-tokens.json` | yes |
| photos | nowhere | — |

Nothing in `./data` is encrypted and an admin can read every profile — see
[SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md). That now includes what somebody eats and what they weigh.
